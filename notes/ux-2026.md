# UX research: what makes 2025–2026's most-shared one-pagers pleasant to use

*Dated 2026-09-15. Complements `quality-gates.md` (jury weights, machine gates, checklist) and `taste.md` (visual craft) — adds sourced external evidence, not repeated there. Every claim below carries a URL.*

## 1. Jury criteria, precisely

**Awwwards** scores every submission on four fixed weights: **Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20%, Content 10%** — Design+Usability alone are 70% of the score. Each site goes to a minimum of 18 jurors; the system auto-drops the 3 scores furthest from the average before computing the result. (https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/) The 2026 jury cohort's public commentary is blunt about motion: heavy-animation sites are "the biggest offenders on accessibility," and studios that talk endlessly about motion but never mention accessibility aren't read as award-mature — judges expect honored reduced-motion, visible focus states, and ~60fps on a mid-range phone as baseline, not bonus. (https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)

**CSS Design Awards** scores UI, UX and Innovation; third-party analysis puts the split near **UI 40% / UX 30% / Innovation 30%** (https://www.webdesignawards.io/awards/cssda). CSSDA's own rule: average judge score **above 8.00** wins Website of the Day; **>6.00 plus 20+ public votes** wins a Public Award; 6–7.99 gets a "Special Kudos" instead. (https://www.cssdesignawards.com/about)

**FWA** runs a panel of 500+ practitioners across 35 countries, scoring out of 100 with a floor of 20 votes to qualify, across Design/Usability/Innovation/Content. (https://thefwa.com/live-judging/) Unlike Awwwards, FWA "tends to reward unconventional, experimental work more aggressively" — the award most tolerant of interaction risk in exchange for real novelty. (https://www.webdesignawards.io/compare/awwwards-vs-fwa)

**Godly** isn't a scored jury: it's editorial curation, self-described as "a curated gallery of the best and most interesting web, app, ui, and visual design work published on the internet" (https://godly.design/), organized by typography-led discovery — it punishes genericness, not a specific formula gap.

Net effect for a one-pager: usability and restraint outweigh raw creativity on two of three scored juries; FWA alone pays a premium for boldness.

## 2. What ordinary users need on a one-pager

NN/g recommends **one sticky Back to Top control per page**, not one per section — "people ignore repetitive links because they are so predictable" — kept small, delayed until the user shows intent to reverse; on section-hopping pages a sticky nav/progress indicator beats Back to Top outright. (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/back-to-top/)

Tap targets: NN/g's floor is **1cm × 1cm (≈44 CSS px)** "to support adequate selection time and prevent fat-finger errors," while the average thumb's contact patch is closer to 2.5cm — bigger than most design systems assume. (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/) Reliable one-handed reach sits in the **bottom 25–40% of the viewport**, where primary mobile CTAs belong. (https://timgraf.com/ux-design/designing-for-the-thumb-zone-a-modern-guide-to-mobile-ux-that-respects-human-anatomy/)

Custom cursors and hijacked scroll are the most litigated "delight" patterns. Baymard's testing found inline/hijacked scroll "annoying to test subjects, who... had to constantly be aware of their cursor position," and on touch devices such areas can trap the user, blocking the page's own scroll entirely. (https://baymard.com/blog/inline-scroll-areas) On cursors, Funka Foundation is sharpest: people rely on **OS-level cursor size/colour accessibility settings**, and "when a website forces its aesthetic choices over their accessibility needs, that's not creative – it's exclusion." (https://stiftelsenfunka.org/about-us/columns/the-curse-of-the-custom-cursor/) Verdict: custom cursor/smooth-scroll are acceptable only as enhancement over working native behavior, never a replacement.

## 3. Performance and CWV in 2026

Field thresholds at the **75th percentile** of real visits: **LCP ≤2.5s** good / 2.5–4.0s needs-improvement / >4.0s poor; **INP ≤200ms** good / 200–500ms needs-improvement / >500ms poor; **CLS ≤0.1** good / 0.1–0.25 needs-improvement / >0.25 poor. INP is field-only — Total Blocking Time is the closest lab proxy. (https://web.dev/articles/vitals)

For image/animation-heavy one-pagers: "a beautiful idle animation does not compensate for a delayed button, menu, or form"; WebGL should be "a garnish, not the whole meal" since it's likeliest to wreck performance if overused. Rough budgets on mid-range hardware: **Canvas 2D ≈3,000 simple draws/frame at 60fps**; **WebGL comfortably 50,000+ instanced primitives/frame** — camera-uniform updates and geometry generation belong off the main thread (Web Workers/OffscreenCanvas) to protect INP. (https://hackernoon.com/the-interaction-budget-keeping-webgl-interfaces-usable-on-real-devices) Tooling: Lighthouse + PageSpeed Insights for lab/field scores, WebPageTest for filmstrip/waterfall diagnosis, `web-vitals` JS for production RUM.

## 4. Accessibility award sites still get wrong

WCAG 2.2 added nine success criteria; one-pagers with sticky chrome/custom scroll violate these most: **2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (AA)** — sticky headers/footers/cookie banners must not hide the focused element; **2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA)** — drag interactions need a single-pointer non-drag alternative; **2.5.8 Target Size (AA)** — pointer targets ≥24×24 CSS px. (https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/; list confirmed at https://testparty.ai/blog/wcag-22-new-success-criteria) Canvas/WebGL scenes routinely fail **1.1.1 Text Alternatives** when copy is drawn as pixels with no DOM equivalent, and reduced-motion is still commonly ignored on scroll-driven heroes — the exact complaint 2026 Awwwards jurors raise (§1).

Tooling: **axe-core** is the rules engine underneath Lighthouse, Pa11y, and most CI plugins, not a standalone app; axe-core+Pa11y together catch roughly a third of real issues automatically, so manual keyboard/screen-reader passes stay mandatory. (https://www.ramotion.com/blog/practical-accessibility-testing-with-pa11y-and-axe-core/) Pa11y runs headless via Puppeteer straight from CI: `pa11y https://example.com`. (https://accessibility-manual.dwp.gov.uk/best-practice/automated-testing-using-axe-core-and-pa11y) Lighthouse's accessibility category runs the same axe rules via `lighthouse <url> --only-categories=accessibility`.

## 5. The "wow that gets shared" factor

Neal Agarwal's **neal.fun** catalogue proves designer-shared and normal-user-shared can be the same object: **Internet Roadtrip, Infinite Craft, Asteroid Launcher, Stimulation Clicker, Cursor Camp, Universe Forecast, Baby Map** are single-screen, single-idea pages with almost no chrome. (https://neal.fun/) Asteroid Launcher's hook is a real instrument — pick a city and asteroid, get crater diameter and casualty estimates from peer-reviewed impact models — and it stays usable because the whole interface is a few sliders and one result panel; nothing else competes for attention.

Among juried sites, Awwwards' **Site of the Year 2025** went to **Messenger** by studio **abeto** — a tiny explorable WebGL planet built around a delivery task — and to **Lando Norris**'s driver site by **OFF+BRAND**. (https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_year/) Both pair one spectacle idea with a conventional, linear scroll underneath, which is why the spectacle doesn't cost usability. From Ukraine, Tubik's **Planetoño** earned Typography Honors on exactly the atlas/space aesthetic this brief favors. (https://www.awwwards.com/websites/Ukraine/)

Pattern across all of them: one idea, one input surface, and the "wow" is a real system (physics, orbital mechanics, delivery logistics) rather than a decorative effect — which is also why they survive contact with people who never open dev tools.

## 6. Typography and Cyrillic craft in Ukrainian sites, 2024–26

Awwwards' Ukraine listing shows steady output from **Tubik** (Planetoño — Typography Honors, Mar 2026; Mosby's Files — Developer Award + Site of the Day, Aug 2026), **Qream** (Darknode — Site of the Day, Mar 2026), **REDSTONE** (Oris Maritime; REDSTONE SOFTWARE), and **BL/S** (Serotoninn — E-commerce Honors). (https://www.awwwards.com/websites/Ukraine/) The typographic throughline: dual-script systems built as one family rather than a Latin face with bolted-on Cyrillic. Kyiv foundry **Contrast Foundry** sells variable families (e.g. CoFo Sans Global, 95 styles) explicitly spanning "Latin, Cyrillic, Variable" as one axis space, not two deliverables. (https://contrastfoundry.com/) For a Cyrillic-heavy one-pager this means budgeting genuinely wider average glyph width and taller lowercase — and checking italics, since variable Cyrillic italics are frequently upright-substituted rather than true obliques — instead of reusing Latin measure/line-height numbers unchanged.

## 7. Test plan — 20 automatable checks

1. `lighthouse <url> --only-categories=performance --chrome-flags="--headless"` — score ≥90.
2. `lighthouse <url> --only-categories=accessibility` — score ≥95.
3. PageSpeed Insights API v5 field data — LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1 at p75. (https://web.dev/articles/vitals)
4. `web-vitals` RUM snippet in production — ≥75% of real visits "Good" on all three metrics.
5. WebPageTest run, Cable/4G profile — Speed Index and visually-complete both ≤~3.4s.
6. `@axe-core/cli <url> --exit` — zero critical/serious violations.
7. `pa11y <url>` (WCAG2AA runner) — zero errors.
8. Lighthouse CI `budgets.json` — JS/image/font payload under set ceilings; CI fails on breach.
9. axe/Lighthouse `color-contrast` audit — ≥4.5:1 body text, ≥3:1 large text/UI, including text over images.
10. DOM query of interactive elements — bounding box ≥24×24 CSS px (WCAG 2.5.8), ≥44×44 for primary CTAs.
11. Static scan of animation triggers — 100% gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.
12. Playwright keyboard-only pass — Tab through entire page; focus never stalls, every control reachable.
13. Playwright focus-visibility check — screenshot `:focus-visible`; outline present, ≥3:1 contrast, not covered by sticky chrome (WCAG 2.4.11).
14. CSS/code audit — native cursor remains or has an equivalent under `prefers-reduced-motion`/forced-colors; never fully hidden without fallback.
15. DOM audit of every `<canvas>`/WebGL element — accessible name or adjacent text equivalent present (WCAG 1.1.1).
16. Lighthouse `layout-shift-elements` — no unsized image/font-swap contributing >0.1 CLS.
17. Font-loading check (WebPageTest waterfall / Lighthouse `font-display`) — critical font preloaded, `swap`/`optional`, zero invisible-text time.
18. Lighthouse `uses-optimized-images`/`modern-image-formats` — above-the-fold image payload ≤~1.5MB, AVIF/WebP.
19. Scripted viewport check, 375–430px widths — primary CTA/nav sit within bottom 40% of screen (thumb zone).
20. Playwright drag-alternative check — every drag interaction has a single-pointer/click alternative (WCAG 2.5.7).
